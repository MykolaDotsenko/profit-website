# A — Evidence-Led Editorial

Diagnostic score: **89/100**

- Farmer-job clarity: **18/20**
- Economic legibility: **19/20**
- Evidence integrity: **15/15**
- Anti-AI-sameness: **12/15**
- Master-brand transfer: **13/15**
- Mobile/static resilience: **9/10**
- Category-confusion containment: **3/5**

## What works

- Strongest evidence hierarchy.
- Definitions and metric identity are unusually clear.
- Negative economics do not get hidden.
- Static/mobile structure is naturally resilient.
- Transfers beyond crop better than a field-map identity because its invariant is evidence/economics, not geometry.
- Supports PROFIT's "quiet technology" doctrine.

## Failure mode

**It can look like a financial report, research publication or consultancy.**

Editorial restraint alone is not a distinctive PROFIT asset.

If the final system relies on:
- serif headline;
- rules;
- ledger/table;
- muted paper palette;

then it is well-designed but still broadly reproducible by many AI-generated premium B2B sites.

## Falsification question

Remove the logo and agricultural nouns.

Would an observer still identify an agricultural-economic decision company, or just a premium finance/research publication?

## Improvement before testing

- Inject one real production-context code that is not generic photography.
- Avoid making "paper/report" the memorable device.
- Keep evidence typography, but develop a more ownable relationship between production context and economics.

# B — Farm Operations Layer

Diagnostic score: **78/100**

- Farmer-job clarity: **16/20**
- Economic legibility: **15/20**
- Evidence integrity: **14/15**
- Anti-AI-sameness: **14/15**
- Master-brand transfer: **9/15**
- Mobile/static resilience: **8/10**
- Category-confusion containment: **2/5**

## What works

- Strongest connection to physical production reality.
- Most obvious "farm → data/context → economics" mechanism.
- Feels less like generic finance/SaaS than A or C.
- Operational nodes create a potentially memorable grammar.
- Strongest candidate for explaining where the number comes from.

## Failure mode

**The field shape can become the identity.**

That creates:
- crop lock;
- GIS/mapping misclassification;
- telemetry/precision-ag expectations;
- false implication that PROFIT already has spatial/machine data capability.

This is the most serious master-brand problem in the current B prototype.

## Falsification question

Replace the field outline with:
- an orchard block/cycle;
- a greenhouse production cycle;
- a pig batch;
- a dairy herd period.

Does the design still feel like the same system?

Current answer:
**partially, but not strongly enough.**

## Improvement before testing

B should be redesigned around an **operational unit/context layer**, not a field polygon.

Potential invariant:
**production unit → attached records/context → economics → evidence → decision**

The production-unit representation can change by domain.

This keeps B's strongest mechanism while removing crop-lock.

# C — Economic Control Room

Diagnostic score: **87/100**

- Farmer-job clarity: **17/20**
- Economic legibility: **20/20**
- Evidence integrity: **14/15**
- Anti-AI-sameness: **12/15**
- Master-brand transfer: **14/15**
- Mobile/static resilience: **8/10**
- Category-confusion containment: **2/5**

## What works

- Strongest immediate visibility of economic state.
- Negative result is impossible to miss.
- Sensitivity and break-even concepts fit naturally.
- Strong master-brand transfer because "economic state" can apply across domain-specific production units.
- Strongest candidate for bad-news/uncertainty communication.
- Makes deterministic economics vs forecasting boundaries easy to expose.

## Failure mode

**It can look more like a mature ERP/control platform than the product actually is.**

Risks:
- overstates shipped product maturity;
- accounting/ERP category confusion;
- dashboard/control-room AI sameness;
- can become visually dense;
- may make "monitoring the farm" feel more central than "supporting a decision".

## Falsification question

If all boxes were replaced with generic SaaS metrics, would the layout still look essentially the same?

Current answer:
**too often yes.**

## Improvement before testing

- Reduce dashboard framing.
- Preserve the strong economic-state concept without presenting a software cockpit.
- Use documentary/production context as a structural counterweight.
- Make the decision question, not the metric grid, the final semantic destination.
