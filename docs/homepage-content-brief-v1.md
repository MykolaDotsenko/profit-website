# 1. Homepage job

The homepage is a **high-trust company presentation and qualified-conversion surface**.

It is not:
- a product dashboard;
- a farm-management application;
- a feature catalogue;
- a technical white paper;
- an investor deck;
- a generic agritech landing page.

The homepage must help a farmer move through this sequence:

**This is relevant to my farm  
→ I understand the economic problem  
→ I understand what PROFIT does  
→ I see one concrete proof  
→ I understand how data becomes economic meaning  
→ I see that uncertainty and evidence are handled honestly  
→ I understand what is current vs future direction  
→ I trust the next step enough to continue**

Primary visitor:
**farmer / farm business decision-maker**

Secondary:
investor / partner / advisor.

The homepage must remain farmer-first even when it contains material useful to investors.

Current production narrative order (owner refinement, 2026-09-27):

**Hero / 30-second model → farmer problem → PROFIT mechanism → detailed Field Profitability proof → current product boundary → whole-farm direction → evidence → farmer control → company/team → pilot CTA.**

Rationale: the hero already contains a compact economic proof object, so the long-form page should establish problem relevance and mechanism before asking a busy farmer to process master-brand breadth. Whole-farm scope remains mandatory, but it must not visually outrank the current concrete wedge. This is an implementation decision, not farmer validation.

# 2. Core communication objective

A visitor should be able to explain PROFIT in plain language after a short visit:

> **PROFIT is being built to connect what happens in agricultural production with what it means economically, so farmers can make decisions with the economics and uncertainty in view.**

This sentence is a **content synthesis**, not a validated final hero line.

The current message test remains WWW-000. Do not bypass it by turning this sentence into an approved hero headline.

# 4. Master narrative

The homepage should express this relationship:

**Production reality  
→ Data / operational context  
→ Economic meaning  
→ Uncertainty / evidence  
→ Decision  
→ Measured outcome**

When actual value evidence exists, the deeper value chain becomes:

**Baseline  
→ Counterfactual  
→ PROFIT-supported intervention  
→ Actual outcome  
→ Incremental economic effect  
→ Attribution  
→ Confidence  
→ Verified Economic Value**

Do not compress those two chains into an unsupported causal story.

---

# 5. Fast layer vs inspectable depth

## Fast layer

Within approximately 5–10 seconds the visitor should understand:

- agriculture / farm context;
- economic decision problem;
- one concrete product proof;
- that the farmer retains decision authority;
- the next action.

## Inspectable depth

A visitor who continues should be able to inspect:

- the economic definition;
- what data goes in;
- what is current vs future;
- evidence state;
- confidence / limitations;
- farmer control;
- methodology;
- company credibility.

Rule:

> **The homepage must feel simple before it feels sophisticated.**

Professional depth should be discoverable, not dumped above the fold.

## 02 — PROFIT in 30 seconds

### Communication job
Give a compact mental model before deeper content.

### Recommended content

**For**  
Farmers and farm businesses across crop, horticulture and livestock production.

**The problem**  
Production, sales and cost records are fragmented. The economic meaning behind the next decision can be hard to see.

**The goal**  
Less guesswork around the economics of a decision — with the farmer still in control.

**First concrete product focus**  
Field Profitability — operating economics field by field. In development.

**The standard**  
Economic value is labelled by evidence. "Verified" is reserved for value that meets the evidence and attribution standard.

**Next step**  
Join the pilot — a short form, then a conversation.

### Density rule
Six short items maximum.

No paragraphs.

## 03 — Farmer problem

### Communication job
Make the visitor recognise the job before explaining technology.

### Primary questions

- Which fields actually make money?
- What does each field really cost once shared costs are allocated?
- At what price or yield would a field break even?
- What should change next season?

The final question should remain explicitly farmer-owned.

### Fragmentation story

The numbers may sit across:
- production records;
- sales/contracts/invoices;
- variable input records;
- machinery/labour/building/land costs;
- weather/market context.

### Product-research insight
Do not imply that the answer requires perfect digitisation.

Professional credibility increases when the site acknowledges:
- incomplete records;
- old machinery;
- intermittent connectivity;
- mixed digital maturity.

### Do not
Turn this into an integrations list.

## 04 — How PROFIT works

### Communication job
Explain the mechanism in a way a farmer can reconstruct.

### Core sequence

**1. Data**  
Start from the records behind a decision, shared with permission.

**2. Economics**  
Turn those records into economic meaning with explicit definitions/formulas.

**3. Decision**  
Put the economics in view. The farmer decides.

**4. Action**  
The action happens on the farm.

**5. Measurement**  
Record what happened in comparable terms.

### Two product-development principles

#### Fit the farm
Start with records the farm already has.

Add automation where it genuinely reduces workload.

Do not assume:
- new machinery;
- telemetry;
- constant connectivity;
- perfect records;
- operators manually entering everything.

#### Keep uncertainty visible
Known economics stay explicit.

Forecast/scenario outputs should expose:
- assumptions;
- range;
- update context;
- confidence;

when evidence supports them.

### Public capability boundary
State clearly:

> These are PROFIT development principles, not a claim that the current Field Profitability build already includes telemetry, forecasting or optimisation.

### Deeper link
Link to company/trust methodology rather than expanding technical detail on homepage.

## 05 — Concrete product proof

### Communication job
Move from broad ambition back to something specific and inspectable.

### Section truth
**Field Profitability · hypothetical example**

### Working title
What field-level economics look like

### What to show
One season, several fields:
- revenue;
- variable costs;
- allocated fixed costs;
- operating costs;
- operating profit;
- per-hectare presentation;
- break-even price/yield where supported by current product truth.

### Definition
**Operating profit = revenue − variable costs − allocated fixed costs.**

It is:
- not gross margin;
- not statutory net profit.

### Required labels
- illustration, not product screenshot;
- hypothetical example;
- confidence not assessed.

### Decision question
Use a decision-oriented prompt:

> What would you investigate on Field 31 before changing the plan?

### Why this matters
The proof should demonstrate:

**show the economics → preserve farmer judgment**

not:

**software tells the farmer what to do.**

### Visual priority
1. economic result;
2. cost/revenue composition;
3. evidence label;
4. decision question;
5. secondary context.

## 07 — Whole-farm scope

### Communication job
Prevent the visitor from interpreting:

**PROFIT = crop-only software**

without implying:

**PROFIT already ships every agricultural domain.**

### Working section concept

**Eyebrow**  
One company · different production systems

**Working title**  
Built around the economics of real agriculture

### Core message
Different production systems need different operating models.

A hectare, orchard block, greenhouse crop cycle, pig batch and dairy herd should not be forced into one unit or one formula.

The shared PROFIT discipline is:

**production reality → context/data → economics → uncertainty/evidence → decision**

### Production contexts to name

**Arable & field crops**  
Field/season economics; current concrete product proof is Field Profitability.

**Horticulture, orchards & berries**  
Block/variety, marketable yield, grade, labour, storage, loss, realised price.

**Vegetables & greenhouse production**  
Crop cycle, labour, energy, inputs, yield, quality, timing and selling price.

**Pig production**  
Feed, growth, mortality, throughput, production cycle and selling conditions.

**Dairy**  
Production, feed, health, reproduction/replacement and price/cost context.

**Other livestock & mixed farms**  
Beef/grazing livestock, poultry/eggs, sheep/goats and mixed production systems with domain-specific units.

### Claim boundary
Use wording such as:

> These production systems describe the direction of the PROFIT master brand. Field Profitability is the current first concrete product focus.

### Statistical evidence
External statistics are **supporting context**, not the farmer-facing value proposition.

Preferred homepage maximum:
**one compact statistical anchor**, if it improves comprehension.

Candidate:

**€531.9B — EU agricultural output in 2024**

Supporting split may appear in small copy:
**€267.7B crops · €218.8B animals and animal products**

Source:
Eurostat, *Key figures on the European food chain — 2025 edition*.

Do not turn this section into a market-size chart or investor story.

### Visual intent
Avoid six identical feature cards.

Prefer one composition showing diversity of production realities while the economic grammar remains constant.

### Do not
- fabricate domain KPIs;
- create fake livestock dashboards;
- use per-hectare units for livestock by default;
- imply product availability;
- use icons as the entire visual identity.

## 08 — Economic value & evidence

### Communication job
Show how PROFIT behaves when evidence is incomplete.

This is a trust section, not a compliance lecture.

### Working title
A value counts only when it can be evidenced

### Evidence ladder

**Hypothetical  
→ Modelled  
→ Observed  
→ Attributed  
→ Verified**

### Confidence

Assessed states:

**High / Medium / Low / Insufficient evidence**

Meta-state:

**Not assessed**

Do not present "Not assessed" as a confidence level.

### Verified Economic Value

VEV should be explained as an evidence standard.

Never imply that:
- forecast = VEV;
- predicted avoided loss = VEV;
- improved outcome automatically = PROFIT-caused value.

### Strong trust line

> PROFIT does not treat a forecast as a fact, a modelled benefit as an outcome, or an outcome as proof that PROFIT caused it.

Use only if it survives final copy review; the underlying principle is LOCKED.

### Visual intent
Evidence/confidence should feel integrated into the brand system, not like legal footnotes.

### Progressive-disclosure rule
Keep the evidence ladder, confidence treatment and current-site status immediately visible. Deeper verification mechanics may sit behind native accessible disclosure, provided the complete content remains available without JavaScript. This preserves the rule: the homepage should feel simple before it feels sophisticated.

## 09 — Trust & farmer control

### Communication job
Answer the objection:

**What happens to my data and who makes the decision?**

### Four primary trust principles

**Permission + purpose**  
Farm data is used with permission and for a stated purpose.

**Farmer decision authority**  
PROFIT supports the decision. It does not take the decision away from the farmer.

**Evidence labels**  
Low/insufficient confidence must stay visible.

**Low-friction first contact**  
Pilot contact form asks for contact/context details — not yields, prices or cost records.

### Deeper questions
Link to:
- what data is needed;
- old machinery;
- incomplete data;
- accuracy;
- ownership;
- low confidence;
- what PROFIT does not do.

### Legal boundary
Do not invent data ownership/security claims while legal terms remain open.

# 8. Data / ML / DSS communication rules

## Data

Public message:
**work with the farm that exists**

The website may explain the philosophy:
- existing records first;
- automation where useful;
- old machinery should not disqualify a farm;
- offline-first where operations require it;

Public message:
**known economics stay explicit; uncertain futures should be shown as scenarios/ranges**

Do not show:
- one black-box future profit;
- unsupported model accuracy;
- deep-learning branding;
- AI prediction theatre.

Public message:
**PROFIT supports a decision; the farmer retains authority**

Core invariant:
**The farmer retains decision authority**

Use:
- decision questions;
- alternatives;
- economic consequences;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- evidence.

Avoid:
- opaque recommendation scores;
- "AI says";
- autonomous prescriptions.

# 10. Visual-content brief

The visual system should make this relationship legible:

**real production → operational context → economics → evidence → decision**

## Documentary agriculture

Show real:
- work;
- animals/crops;
- machinery;
- materials;
- seasonal conditions;
- production constraints.

Avoid synthetic perfection.

## Economic layer

Use:
- deliberate typography;
- tabular numerals where appropriate;
- units with clear semantics;
- definitions;
- evidence/confidence labels.

Do not use giant metrics purely for visual drama.

## Technology layer

Technology should feel:
- precise;
- quiet;
- useful;
- subordinate to the farmer/economic story.

Avoid visualising "AI" as a glowing object.

## Motion

Static composition must work first.

Motion may explain:
- information lineage;
- continuity;
- decision sequence.

Motion must not:
- create unsupported causality;
- delay evidence;
- hide meaning;
- become the remembered object instead of the economic idea.

# 11. Anti-AI-sameness requirements

For WWW-001 art directions:

- at least three genuinely independent compositions;
- no shared editorial DOM as mandatory scaffold;
- same underlying message/proof/evidence controls;
- equivalent fidelity;
- static first;
- logo-off/category-confusion challenge;
- master-brand transfer across crop, horticulture/greenhouse and livestock contexts;
- first-good-concept quarantine;
- independent evaluation before convergence.

Reject a direction as master-brand candidate if:
- it requires field geometry to feel like PROFIT;
- it looks like generic SaaS after removing the logo;
- it depends on stock/synthetic agriculture;
- it relies on AI effects rather than product logic;
- it loses evidence/confidence semantics;
- it makes the product look more shipped/proven than it is.

# 13. Content density guardrails

Homepage target:

- one primary idea per section;
- short lead paragraphs;
- one evidence/statistic moment per section maximum;
- no long research summaries;

# 14. Conversion logic

Primary conversion today:
**qualified pilot conversation**

Do not optimise for raw form volume.

Quality signals:
- visitor understood the economic job;
- visitor understood current product status;
- visitor accepts the evidence/uncertainty posture;
- relevant farm context;
- willingness to discuss pilot/problem.

Future measurement:
- comprehension;
- qualified pilot interest;
- form completion;
- farmer vs investor paths;
- message recall;
- trust/misclassification failures.

Do not optimise vanity engagement alone.

# 16. Homepage acceptance test

A production candidate should pass the following.

## Farmer comprehension
After 10 seconds, can the farmer answer:
- What is PROFIT?
- What economic problem does it address?
- Is the current product about fields, the whole farm, or both?
- What would I do next?

## Product truth
Can the visitor distinguish:
- master-brand direction;
- current Field Profitability focus;
- hypothetical example;
- unshipped future capabilities?

## Trust
Can the visitor tell:
- what is evidence vs illustration;
- who makes the decision;
- whether forecasts are facts;
- whether farm data is needed before first contact?

## Brand
Without the logo:
- does the system still feel connected to real agricultural economics?
- does it avoid generic AI/SaaS cues?
- can the grammar transfer beyond fields?

## UX
- strong at 390 / 768 / 1024 / 1440;
- meaningful without motion;
- reduced-motion parity;
- WCAG 2.2 AA target;
- no core information hidden behind JS;
- no obvious performance regression.
