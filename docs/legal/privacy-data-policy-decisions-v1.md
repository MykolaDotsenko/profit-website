# PROFIT Privacy & Farm-Data Policy Decisions v1

Status: **Internal policy decision — controller-specific legal approval still required**
Date: **2026-09-27**
Scope: public website pilot enquiries and the defaults for any later farm-data pilot agreement.

## 1. Pilot-enquiry purpose

Purpose: **reply to an inbound pilot enquiry and assess whether the current pilot is a plausible fit.**

The five fields are limited to name, farm/company, country, email and farm type.

Not part of this purpose:
- marketing newsletters;
- advertising audiences;
- cross-customer analytics;
- model training;
- automated eligibility decisions;
- collection of farm production records.

## 2. Proposed GDPR legal basis

Proposed basis for the minimal inbound-enquiry flow: **legitimate interests under GDPR Article 6(1)(f)**, subject to final controller-specific legal approval and a documented balancing assessment.

Rationale:
- the person initiates contact and reasonably expects a reply;
- only five low-risk contact/context fields are requested;
- the data is used only to reply and assess fit;
- no marketing or unrelated secondary use follows from the enquiry;
- no automated decision with legal/significant effect is made;
- the person must have an objection/privacy-rights route in the final notice.

If final counsel concludes that pre-contractual steps under Article 6(1)(b) are more appropriate for a defined pilot application, the final notice must reflect the actual purpose/relationship. Do not mix bases casually.

## 3. Pilot-enquiry retention

Policy target for enquiries that do not enter a pilot/customer relationship:
**delete or anonymise personal contact data 12 months after the last substantive contact**, unless a documented legal, dispute, compliance or requested follow-up need requires a different period.

If the person enters a pilot or contract, the applicable pilot/customer terms replace this enquiry-only retention rule for the records necessary to that relationship.

## 4. Marketing

Submitting the pilot form does **not** opt a person into marketing.

Any future marketing subscription must have its own transparent basis and opt-in/opt-out mechanism as required.

## 5. Automated decisions

Current policy: **no solely automated decision-making or profiling with legal or similarly significant effects in the pilot-enquiry flow.**

Fit assessment may be supported by structured criteria, but a human remains responsible for the response.

## 6. Farm-data default purpose

Farm records may be requested only after fit/purpose/terms are established and only when each requested record category has a stated product/pilot purpose.

General pilot participation is not permission for unrelated reuse.

## 7. Secondary use, benchmarking and model training

Default: **not permitted by pilot participation alone.**

No cross-customer benchmarking, model training or unrelated research use is implied.

If PROFIT later wants a secondary purpose, it must be separately defined, documented and supported by the appropriate contractual/legal basis and farmer control.

## 8. Farm-data access

Default: least-privilege access limited to people/services necessary to deliver the agreed pilot purpose.

Actual roles, processors and subprocessors must be listed in the final terms once the production infrastructure is known.

## 9. Farm-data correction and provenance

Material recorded, imported, inferred and modelled values must remain distinguishable where relevant to decisions/evidence.

The farmer must have a route to flag/correct materially incorrect records.

## 10. Export / return / deletion

At pilot exit, the farmer should be able to obtain agreed exportable records/results in a usable common format where technically feasible.

Policy targets after a valid deletion/end-of-pilot trigger:
- active systems: delete within **30 days**;
- backups: expire/delete within **90 days** where immediate granular deletion is not technically feasible;
- longer retention: only where legally required or explicitly documented.

These are product/contract targets. Final terms must match the actual storage and backup architecture before they are promised publicly.

## 11. Security and incident wording

Only implemented controls may be described. No unearned certification or generic 'bank-grade' wording.

Processor obligations, incident handling and notification duties must match the actual architecture and applicable law.

## 12. Facts still required before release

The policy decisions above do not replace these facts:
- controller legal identity and registration details;
- monitored controller/privacy contact;
- DPO applicability/contact if required;
- actual hosting/form/email/CRM processors;
- actual storage/processing locations and international-transfer mechanism if any;
- implemented security controls and log retention;
- competent supervisory authority based on controller establishment;
- final legal approval of the notice and farm-data terms.

## 13. Primary legal references

- GDPR Article 5 principles and Articles 12–14 transparency requirements.
- GDPR Article 6 lawful bases.
- European Commission guidance on GDPR principles, legal grounds, controller/processor roles and data-subject information.

## 14. Release decision

Keep privacy-notice and data-terms BLOCKED until the external facts and formal approval above exist.

This document narrows those gates; it does not close them.
