# Avoiding AI Sameness — PROFIT Standard

Research date: 2026-09-26
Status: Design / Brand / AI Governance Standard

This document is the result of a five-pass review of current research, brand-system guidance, web-design practice, and agritech category patterns.

The objective is not merely to make PROFIT look different.

The objective is to build a recognisable, evidence-led brand system that AI can accelerate without flattening into generic agritech/SaaS output.

---

# Executive conclusion

AI sameness is a real risk, but it is not solved by a better prompt.

The strongest protection is a system:

**proprietary inputs**
+
**divergent human framing**
+
**distinctive brand codes**
+
**controlled AI generation**
+
**governance**
+
**blind recognition testing**

PROFIT must never use AI as the source of its creative point of view.

AI may:
- expand;
- adapt;
- test;
- productionise;
- scale;

but the distinctive idea must come from PROFIT's real domain, evidence model, farm context, and brand strategy.

# Pass 1 — Evidence: is AI sameness actually real?

## Finding 1: collective homogenisation exists

A 2026 systematic review and three-level meta-analysis of human–AI co-creation research found a small but statistically significant homogenisation effect across 19 studies / 61 effect sizes.

The effect was task-sensitive and stronger in more semantically constrained ideation tasks.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1080/0144929X.2026.2726451

## Finding 2: AI can improve individual output while reducing collective novelty

Doshi & Hauser (Science Advances, 2024) found that access to generative-AI ideas improved evaluated creativity/quality for individual writers, but AI-assisted outputs became more similar to one another.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290

## Finding 3: AI can create design fixation

A CHI 2024 experiment found that designers exposed to AI-generated imagery showed more fixation on initial examples and produced fewer ideas, with less variety and lower originality than the baseline group.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642919

## Finding 4: workflow matters

2026 research on diverse AI personas suggests homogenisation is not necessarily unavoidable: deliberately diverse prompting/personas can reduce convergence.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2026.100289

### PROFIT conclusion

The main creative risk is **premature convergence**:

AI produces a polished first answer.
The team reacts to that answer instead of exploring the problem space.
Later variants become variations of the same initial concept.

### Rule

**No AI-generated visual becomes the first reference point for a major brand decision.**

For:
- homepage art direction;
- logo/symbol;
- signature visual motif;
- photography treatment;
- motion identity;

the divergent stage must begin from the brief, category analysis, real farm material and human concept exploration.

# Pass 3 — Distinctiveness: what should replace generic cues?

Ehrenberg-Bass research defines strong distinctive assets by two properties:
- uniqueness;
- fame/prevalence.

For a new brand, fame starts low. Therefore PROFIT should prioritise assets with strong **potential uniqueness**, then consistently build memory links.

Source:
https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brands-of-distinction

## Candidate PROFIT distinctive assets

### 1. Economic typography

Own:
- €/ha;
- margin;
- cost;
- revenue;
- yield economics;
- confidence;
- evidence period.

Design these as a recognisable typographic language.

### 2. Field geometry

Use:
- parcel outlines;
- operational paths;
- zones;
- real field shapes;

as a graphic system, not generic map decoration.

### 3. Reality → Data → Economics composition

Signature visual construction:

**real agricultural artifact**
→
**precise data layer**
→
**economic interpretation**

Examples:
- field photo → boundary/operation → margin;
- tractor task → hours/input/cost → €/ha;
- herd/feed context → production data → economic outcome.

### 4. Evidence language

Canonical states:
- Modelled
- Observed
- Attributed
- Verified

### 5. Confidence language

Canonical states:
- High
- Medium
- Low
- Insufficient evidence

### 6. Calm motion grammar

Motion:
- reveals causality;
- follows data flow;
- preserves spatial continuity;
- avoids decorative bounce/noise.

### 7. Real agricultural photography

Photography should show:
- work;
- equipment;
- crop/soil;
- actual operational context;
- imperfection;
- physical texture.

It should not look like generic corporate agriculture stock.

## Important correction

Do not assume that "field geometry" or "green" is automatically distinctive.

A brand asset is weak if competitors can use the same cue just as naturally.

Ehrenberg-Bass explicitly warns that meaning-based asset selection can reduce distinctiveness when many brands choose the same obvious category cue.

### Rule

**Category relevance is not the same as brand distinctiveness.**

# Pass 4 — Process: how AI should be used without collapsing creativity

## 4.1 Divergent-first protocol

Before AI generation, create at least three concept territories from different strategic starting points.

For PROFIT:

### Territory A — Economic Evidence
Start from:
- margin;
- VEV;
- confidence;
- attribution.

### Territory B — Farm Reality
Start from:
- field;
- operator;
- machinery;
- soil;
- crop.

### Territory C — Decision Intelligence
Start from:
- alternatives;
- decision;
- consequence;
- measured outcome.

These must be different ideas, not color/layout variants.

## 4.2 No one-shot creative direction

Do not ask:

"Design a premium modern agritech website."

That prompt imports the model's category average.

Instead provide:
- exact audience;
- business problem;
- brand belief;
- non-negotiable evidence rules;
- proprietary visual ingredients;
- exclusions;
- intended emotional response;
- competitor reference/exclusion context.

## 4.3 Separate divergent and convergent stages

### Divergent
Goal:
expand concept space.

Rules:
- multiple starting metaphors;
- multiple visual structures;
- no scoring too early;
- no "make this version better";
- actively search for opposites.

### Convergent
Goal:
select and refine.

Criteria:
- farmer comprehension;
- trust;
- uniqueness;
- product truth;
- system scalability;
- technical feasibility.

Research on structured divergent/convergent AI workflows supports this separation as a way to reduce fixation.

Reference:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18388

## 4.4 Use diverse AI roles, not one default assistant

For exploration, ask independent roles to propose concepts:

- agricultural documentary art director;
- financial-information designer;
- editorial brand designer;
- cartographic/data visualisation designer;
- industrial/product photographer;
- skeptical farmer;
- brand-distinctiveness reviewer.

Do not let later roles see earlier outputs during the first divergence round.

This reduces correlated starting points.

## 4.5 Ground AI in proprietary inputs

The best anti-sameness input is not more adjectives.

Use:
- real PROFIT product screens;
- actual field shapes;
- real farm photography;
- real economic units;
- real methodology;
- real evidence-state system;
- real team/farm language;
- approved typography/color/motion tokens.

Adobe's 2026 brand-AI guidance similarly emphasises curated, high-quality, brand-specific source assets rather than generic model priors.

Sources:
https://business.adobe.com/uk/blog/developing-a-brand-specific-custom-ai-model
https://business.adobe.com/blog/training-custom-ai-models-on-brand-visual-identity

## 4.6 Quality over quantity

Do not train/reference every asset ever produced.

Create an **Approved Brand Corpus** containing only:
- best examples;
- current design era;
- internally consistent visual language;
- legally usable assets;
- strategically desired behaviors.

Bad or outdated assets teach drift.

## 4.7 Maintain an explicit exclusion set

Create a live **NOT PROFIT** board.

Examples:
- neon AI glow;
- generic abstract networks;
- farmer-with-tablet stock;
- green-gradient SaaS;
- cartoon agriculture;
- playful bouncing metrics;
- "AI-powered" hero;
- generic bento homepage;
- fake futuristic farm imagery.

AI prompts and human reviews should use this exclusion set.

# Pass 5 — Governance and testing: how to prevent drift after launch

The long-term risk is not only one generic homepage.

It is **brand drift**:
each AI-generated page/campaign/component deviates slightly until the brand becomes incoherent.

2026 research describes "agentic brand drift" as gradual unintended divergence that can occur even while performance metrics improve.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bushor.2026.05.013

## 5.1 Canonical Brand Source of Truth

Maintain one machine- and human-readable source with:

### Strategy
- audience;
- problem;
- category hypothesis;
- brand belief;
- value proposition;
- evidence philosophy.

### Verbal
- voice;
- tone matrix;
- preferred terminology;
- prohibited phrases;
- examples.

### Visual
- typography;
- colors;
- geometry;
- photography;
- icon/diagram rules;
- motion.

### Evidence
- VEV terminology;
- evidence states;
- confidence states;
- claim rules.

### Exclusions
- category clichés;
- competitor-like patterns;
- rejected styles.

### Decision rationale
Store **why** important rules exist, not only the rule itself.

AI systems need the reasoning context to avoid mechanically applying rules in the wrong situation.

## 5.2 AI may generate only inside guardrails

Workflow:

**brief**
→
**brand context**
→
**approved source assets**
→
**AI variants**
→
**brand audit**
→
**human creative decision**
→
**responsive/accessibility verification**
→
**merge/publish**

Never:

**prompt → publish**

## 5.3 No auto-merge for brand-critical changes

Require human review for:
- homepage hero;
- logo/symbol;
- headline;
- typography;
- color system;
- photography style;
- motion language;
- evidence visualization;
- new product/module identity.

## 5.4 Brand-lint checklist

For every new page/major asset:

- Does it use a recognised PROFIT brand code?
- Does it introduce an unapproved new aesthetic?
- Could it belong to a competitor?
- Are the typography and numeric styles canonical?
- Are evidence/confidence states canonical?
- Is the photography on-brand?
- Is the copy recognisably PROFIT?
- Is motion consistent?
- Is any generic AI imagery present?
- Is every claim evidence-appropriate?

## 5.5 Blind logo-off test

Hide:
- logo;
- company name.

Show the design to target users.

Ask:
- Which assets look like the same company?
- Which feel like PROFIT?
- What clues caused that judgment?

This tests actual distinctiveness rather than designer intention.

## 5.6 Competitor confusion test

Mix:
- PROFIT concepts;
- competitor screenshots;
- generic AI/SaaS mockups.

Ask:
- Which belong together?
- Which brand seems economically focused?
- Which seems agronomic?
- Which seems generic AI?
- Which do you remember later?

Goal:
PROFIT should form a coherent cluster distinct from competitors.

## 5.7 24-hour recall test

After exposure, ask users the next day:
- What do you remember?
- Which visual cue?
- Which phrase?
- Which metric?
- What did PROFIT do?

A beautiful site with no memory trace is weak branding.

## 5.8 Distinctive Asset Grid later

As awareness grows, formally measure candidate assets for:
- uniqueness;
- fame/prevalence.

Do not call an element a "distinctive asset" merely because the design team likes it.

# PROFIT AI Sameness Risk Model

Do not use a pseudo-precise numerical score.

Classify risk qualitatively.

## LOW RISK

The asset:
- uses proprietary farm/product inputs;
- expresses canonical brand codes;
- is recognisably PROFIT without the logo;
- survives competitor comparison;
- has human art direction;
- uses AI mainly for execution/adaptation.

## MEDIUM RISK

The asset:
- uses a known web pattern;
- contains some PROFIT codes;
- relies partly on generic AI output;
- could plausibly resemble competitors.

Requires redesign/review before becoming a key brand surface.

## HIGH RISK

The asset:
- starts from generic prompting;
- follows current SaaS/agritech visual clichés;
- has no proprietary content;
- contains no PROFIT-specific evidence/economic language;
- looks polished but interchangeable.

Do not publish as a brand-defining surface.

# PROFIT Anti-Sameness Creative Protocol

## Step 0 — Category deconstruction
Before concepting, collect current competitor/category examples.

Document:
- common headlines;
- colors;
- photography;
- UI structures;
- motion;
- claims;
- iconography.

Create an exclusion board.

## Step 1 — Define the ownable truth
Answer:

**What can only PROFIT credibly say/show because of our product philosophy and evidence system?**

Current strongest hypothesis:

**Economic decision intelligence measured through Verified Economic Value.**

## Step 2 — Generate 3+ independent concept territories
No shared first visual.

## Step 3 — Use real/proprietary inputs
Farm photos, field geometry, real economic structures, product UI, evidence model.

## Step 4 — AI divergence
Generate independent routes/personas.
Do not refine the first good output immediately.

## Step 5 — Human convergence
Select based on:
- truth;
- trust;
- distinctiveness;
- usefulness;
- scalability.

Not merely beauty.

## Step 6 — Systemise
Turn chosen ideas into:
- tokens;
- components;
- photography rules;
- copy rules;
- motion rules;
- examples;
- exclusions.

## Step 7 — Red-team similarity
Compare against:
- competitors;
- generic SaaS;
- generic AI-generated sites.

## Step 8 — Blind user test
Logo-off + recall + comprehension.

## Step 9 — Governance
AI only generates from the approved system.

## Step 10 — Re-audit quarterly or after major brand/product changes
Check:
- drift;
- category convergence;
- asset recognition;
- new clichés.

# Specific design implications for profit-website

## Hero

Reject:
- generic field + floating glass cards;
- AI gradient + abstract nodes;
- satellite map + "AI-powered insights".

Prefer:
- real agricultural artifact;
- one precise economic result;
- PROFIT evidence language;
- product/farm connection.

## Product proof

Reject:
- mosaic of tiny screenshots.

Prefer:
- one decision;
- one field;
- one economic consequence;
- one evidence state.

## Photography

Prefer owned/commissioned/real farm imagery.

AI-generated agriculture should not be used as factual proof of real operations.

If AI imagery is used for conceptual brand work, it must be clearly non-documentary and conform to the approved visual system.

## Copy

Avoid category-average language.

Prioritise:
- margin;
- cost;
- economic effect;
- baseline;
- counterfactual;
- confidence;
- attribution;
- verified value.

## Motion

Avoid fashionable motion signatures copied from current SaaS sites.

Build one causal motion grammar:
**reality → measurement → economic meaning**.

# Kill criteria

Reject a design direction if:

- the strongest reason to choose it is "it looks modern";
- it can be reproduced by a generic "premium agritech website" prompt;
- it lacks a proprietary PROFIT input;
- it resembles category leaders more than PROFIT's own codes;
- users cannot distinguish it in a logo-off test;
- the design requires the logo to create all recognition;
- novelty harms farmer trust or comprehension;
- AI is making the major creative decisions rather than accelerating a defined direction.

---

# What we should deliberately keep stable

Once validated, avoid unnecessary rebrands of:

- economic-number language;
- evidence states;
- confidence language;
- field/data geometry;
- core typography roles;
- photography principles;
- motion grammar.

Ehrenberg-Bass guidance strongly cautions against changing distinctive assets without strategic reason.

Brand recognition compounds through consistency.

---

# Strongest final conclusion

The best defense against AI sameness is not anti-AI.

It is **high-context AI inside a distinctive human-designed system**.

For PROFIT:

**real farm truth**
+
**economic decision philosophy**
+
**VEV/evidence system**
+
**proprietary visual codes**
+
**divergent-first creative process**
+
**human creative ownership**
+
**governed AI scaling**
+
**recognition testing**

If AI is asked to invent PROFIT from scratch, it will regress toward the category average.

If AI is given a mature PROFIT system, it can scale distinctiveness instead of erasing it.

## Pass 6 — Human judgement must own the creative process

D&AD's 2026 AI for Creativity curriculum contains explicit modules on:
- AI Sameness;
- Creative Integrity;
- The Human Advantage;
- Strategic Framing;
- Contextual Intelligence;
- Mischievous Thinking;
- Taste & Judgement.

It treats AI as a collaborator with multiple roles:
- Challenger;
- Explorer;
- Analyst;
- Builder;
- Simulator.

### PROFIT conclusion

The dangerous pattern is one universal AI assistant that:
- frames the brief;
- generates the concept;
- chooses the winner;
- writes the copy;
- creates the visuals;
- evaluates itself.

That collapses independent judgement into one statistical prior.

### Mandatory role separation

Use AI in explicit roles.

#### Challenger
Purpose:
attack assumptions and identify category clichés.

Allowed:
- "What about this concept looks generic?"
- "Which parts resemble standard agritech/SaaS patterns?"
- "What assumption are we taking for granted?"

Not allowed:
select the final creative direction.

#### Explorer
Purpose:
expand conceptual possibilities after the human brief exists.

Allowed:
- generate alternative metaphors;
- find adjacent references;
- propose unusual combinations.

#### Analyst
Purpose:
compare against:
- competitor patterns;
- brand constraints;
- accessibility/performance limits;
- research evidence.

#### Builder
Purpose:
productionise an already selected direction.

Allowed:
- responsive variants;
- component implementations;
- image adaptations;
- layout production.

#### Simulator
Purpose:
simulate reactions from different viewpoints.

Examples:
- skeptical farmer;
- investor;
- agronomist;
- non-technical visitor.

### Human-only ownership

Humans retain final ownership of:
- strategic framing;
- creative point of view;
- brand codes;
- taste;
- final selection;
- exceptions to the system.

### New rule

**AI can occupy one creative role at a time. It must not be brief writer, creator, judge, and approver in the same loop.**

## Pass 7 — Protect divergence before refinement

D&AD's Creative Collider teaches repeatable ideation and explicitly highlights the "secret power of solo vs plenary thinking."

D&AD's Art & Science of Ideas separates:
- Think it;
- Contextualise it;
- Create it;
- Push it;
- Refine it.

Current human-AI research also shows that linear chat workflows can encourage premature convergence, while explicit divergent and convergent stages reduce fixation.

A 2026 Design Society study found that AI could improve usability of ideas but also hinder human creativity and reduce idea generation in the divergent phase.

### PROFIT conclusion

A polished first answer is dangerous because it creates an anchor.

### Mandatory divergence protocol

#### Phase A — Solo framing
Before seeing AI concepts, each involved human writes:
- the problem;
- the ownable truth;
- 2–3 tensions;
- one unexpected direction.

Do not share yet.

#### Phase B — Independent concept territories
Create at least three genuinely different territories.

They must differ in:
- underlying idea;
- visual metaphor;
- information hierarchy;
- emotional register.

Color changes do not count.

#### Phase C — AI expansion
Use AI to expand each territory separately.

Do not ask:
"Which of these is best?"

Do ask:
"What unexplored direction exists within this territory?"

#### Phase D — Forced push
Before selection, push promising ideas one step further.

Ask:
- What would make this less expected?
- What could be removed?
- What if we invert the hierarchy?
- What proprietary PROFIT material could replace generic imagery?

#### Phase E — Convergence
Only after divergence is documented:
- evaluate;
- combine if justified;
- choose;
- refine.

### First-good-concept quarantine

Do not polish the first acceptable AI concept immediately.

Before refinement begins, the concept space must contain multiple independent strategic options.

### New rule

**Breadth before beauty.**

## Pass 8 — Replace taste arguments with a creative evaluation framework

D&AD's Creative Evaluation workshop explicitly targets:
- a shared language for evaluating creativity;
- clear benchmarks for creative excellence/effectiveness;
- frameworks that can also assess AI output.

### PROFIT implication

"Looks premium" and "I like version B" are not adequate review criteria.

### PROFIT Creative Evaluation Compass

This is a PROFIT-specific framework; it is not claimed to reproduce D&AD's proprietary Creative Compass.

For each major creative direction, review eight questions:

#### 1. Product Truth
Does the idea emerge from something true about PROFIT?

#### 2. Farmer Relevance
Does it represent a real farmer problem/outcome?

#### 3. Distinctiveness Potential
Could this become recognisably PROFIT with repetition?

#### 4. Category Contrast
Does it avoid collapsing into generic agritech/SaaS cues?

#### 5. Evidence Integrity
Does the creative treatment preserve evidence, uncertainty, and VEV semantics?

#### 6. Comprehension
Can a visitor understand the core message quickly?

#### 7. System Potential
Can the idea scale across:
- website;
- product;
- presentations;
- reports;
- social;
- future modules?

#### 8. Execution Quality
Can it be implemented with high accessibility, performance, and craft?

### Review states

Avoid pseudo-precise /100 scoring.

Use:
- PASS;
- CONCERN;
- REJECT;
- NEEDS EVIDENCE.

### Blind review rule

Where practical, reviewers should first assess concepts without knowing:
- who created them;
- whether they came from AI;
- which person internally prefers them.

This reduces status/tool bias.

## Pass 9 — Make the brand system machine-readable

Figma's 2026 LLM Context Design guidance identifies three layers required for reliable AI use:

1. tokens;
2. explicit specs / usage rules;
3. an audit loop.

The key lesson is that a human can infer missing context; an LLM guesses.

### PROFIT implication

A traditional PDF guideline is insufficient for AI-assisted production.

### Required AI-readable context package

Create a version-controlled context layer that includes:

#### Tokens
Examples:
- color roles;
- typography roles;
- spacing;
- radii;
- motion durations/easing;
- evidence colors;
- confidence styles.

#### Component specs
For each component:
- what it is;
- when to use;
- when not to use;
- required content;
- allowed variants;
- accessibility behavior;
- responsive behavior.

#### Brand rules
Examples:
- preferred vocabulary;
- banned category-average phrases;
- evidence terminology;
- photography rules;
- numeric formatting;
- motion grammar.

#### Relationships
The system must encode not only values, but relationships.

Example:
- "verified" may only appear when VEV criteria are met;
- "primary CTA" exists once per major decision surface;
- field geometry is supporting context, not decorative wallpaper;
- green is not sufficient to indicate positive economic value without accessible redundant cues.

#### Examples
Include:
- approved examples;
- rejected examples;
- explanation of why.

### Frozen zones

Figma's prompt guidance recommends explicitly stating what must not change during exploration.

For PROFIT, define three classes:

#### LOCKED
AI cannot change without human approval:
- evidence terminology;
- core brand codes;
- canonical units;
- accessibility constraints;
- logo/symbol;
- approved type roles.

#### FLEXIBLE
AI may explore within constraints:
- section composition;
- crop choices;
- editorial rhythm;
- supporting illustration;
- micro-layout.

#### OPEN
AI may diverge widely:
- early concept metaphors;
- non-production moodboards;
- exploratory references.

### Audit loop

Every week or every meaningful batch of AI-generated work:
- sample recent outputs;
- compare against tokens/specs;
- identify drift;
- update context if the drift reveals a genuine missing rule.

### New rule

**If AI repeatedly gets something wrong, do not keep correcting the output. Fix the context/system that allowed the guess.**

## Pass 10 — Curated training/reference data + distinctive asset memory building

Adobe's current custom-model guidance makes several useful points:
- custom models need approved brand assets, not only written guidelines;
- quality outweighs quantity;
- training sets should be stylistically coherent;
- compositional variety should exist inside that coherent language;
- outdated or conflicting design eras should not be mixed;
- IP and usage rights must be explicit;
- human review remains essential.

Adobe currently describes small, focused custom-model training sets (often 10–30 images for a single concept).

### PROFIT implication

Do **not** rush to train a custom image model now.

If the brand language is not yet validated, a custom model would simply scale immature decisions.

### Custom-model readiness criteria

Only consider brand-specific model training after:
- visual direction is stable;
- photography treatment is approved;
- a meaningful corpus of high-quality owned/licensed examples exists;
- brand codes have been used repeatedly;
- user testing supports the direction;
- rights/provenance are clear.

### Approved Brand Corpus v2

Organise by concept, not one giant folder.

Possible collections:
- Farm Photography / Fields
- Farm Photography / Machinery & Operators
- Field Geometry
- Economic Typography
- Evidence Visualisation
- Product Exhibits
- Motion Keyframes / Storyboards

For each asset record:
- status;
- purpose;
- rights/provenance;
- why approved;
- channel;
- visual attributes;
- exclusions.

### Important brand-memory correction

Ehrenberg-Bass research says distinctive assets become strong through repeated linkage with the brand name.

Therefore:

**Logo-off recognition is a diagnostic goal, not a production rule for a young brand.**

Early PROFIT assets should usually be **co-presented with the PROFIT name/logo** so the memory link can form.

### Asset-building sequence

1. Choose very few candidate assets.
2. Pair them consistently with PROFIT.
3. Use them prominently across touchpoints.
4. Resist unnecessary changes.
5. Measure recognition/uniqueness later.
6. Only then reduce reliance on the brand name where evidence supports it.

### New rule

**One strong repeated brand code is more valuable than ten fashionable ones.**

# New AI-Sameness Red-Team Checklist

Before approving any major concept, ask:

### Origin
- Did the concept begin from PROFIT-specific truth or from AI aesthetics?
- Was human framing completed before generative exploration?

### Divergence
- Were there multiple independent territories?
- Did we push beyond the first acceptable solution?
- Did solo thinking happen before group convergence?

### Category
- Which current competitors or category patterns does this resemble?
- Is that resemblance strategically useful or accidental?

### Brand
- Which specific PROFIT code is being strengthened?
- Is the code unique enough to merit long-term investment?
- Are we trying to own an obvious category cue that competitors can own equally well?

### AI process
- What role did AI play?
- Did AI also judge its own output?
- Did it have the current tokens/specs/exclusions?
- What did a human contribute that the model could not infer?

### Evidence
- Does the design preserve the difference between modelled, observed, attributed, and verified?
- Does visual drama overstate confidence?

### Memory
- Is the new code paired clearly with the PROFIT name?
- Are we repeating a stable code or inventing another new one?

### System
- Could the same rule work in product UI, reports, investor material, and future modules?
- If not, is this intentionally campaign-specific?
