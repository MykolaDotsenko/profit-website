# 03 — Whole-farm scope

Eyebrow:

**One economic discipline · different production systems**

Title:

**Different farms. Different production models. The same economic discipline.**

Lead:

**A hectare, an orchard block, a greenhouse crop cycle, a pig batch and a dairy herd are not interchangeable. PROFIT is being built to keep each production reality specific while making its economics explicit.**

Production contexts:

### Arable & field crops
**Field- and season-level economics across yield, price, inputs, machinery and allocated costs. Field Profitability is the current first concrete focus.**

### Horticulture, orchards & berries
**Block, variety, marketable yield, grade, labour, storage, losses and realised price create a different economic model from field crops.**

### Vegetables & greenhouse production
**Crop cycle, labour, energy, inputs, quality, timing and selling price shape the economics of protected and open-field production.**

### Pig production
**Feed, growth, mortality, throughput, batch performance and selling conditions shape the economics of a production cycle.**

### Dairy
**Milk production, feed, herd health, reproduction, replacement and price/cost context interact at cow, group and herd level.**

### Other livestock & mixed farms
**Beef cattle, poultry, eggs, sheep, goats and mixed farms need domain-specific production units — not a forced per-hectare model.**

Boundary note:

**These production systems describe the direction of the PROFIT master brand. They are not a claim that every domain is a current product. Field Profitability is the first concrete product focus.**

# 05 — Farmer problem

Eyebrow:

**Start with the decision**

Title:

**The hard part is rarely one missing number**

Lead:

**A farm decision can depend on records created in different places, at different times and for different purposes.**

Questions:

### Which fields actually make money?
**Bring revenue and allocated costs into the same operating-profit view.**

### What does each field really cost once shared costs are allocated?
**Make variable and allocated fixed costs visible in the same definition.**

### At what price or yield would a field break even?
**Show the break-even point from the recorded economics.**

### What should change next season?
**That remains your call. PROFIT should make the economics and uncertainty easier to inspect before you decide.**

Why the answer is hard to see:

- **Production** — yields and harvest records
- **Sales** — prices, contracts and invoices
- **Variable costs** — seed, fertiliser, crop protection, fuel
- **Fixed costs** — machinery, labour, buildings, land
- **Outside factors** — weather and markets

Conclusion:

**The work is not collecting data for its own sake. It is bringing the right records together so the economic question can be inspected consistently.**

# 06 — How PROFIT works

Eyebrow:

**How PROFIT works**

Title:

**From farm records to an economic decision**

Lead:

**Five steps. Known economics stay explicit. The farmer keeps decision authority.**

### 01 — Data · You
**Start from the records behind a decision, shared with your permission.**

Field Profitability example:
**Crop, area, yield, price, variable costs and allocated fixed costs for one field and season.**

### 02 — Economics · PROFIT
**Apply explicit definitions and repeatable formulas to turn records into economic meaning.**

Field Profitability example:
**Revenue, operating costs, operating profit, per-hectare figures, break-even price and yield.**

### 03 — Decision · You
**Inspect the economics, assumptions and available evidence before deciding what to do.**

Field Profitability example:
**Compare saved fields side by side before changing the plan.**

### 04 — Action · You
**The action happens on the farm, not in software.**

### 05 — Measurement · You + PROFIT
**Record the outcome in comparable terms so the economic effect can be checked rather than assumed.**

Field Profitability example:
**The next season is recorded with the same definitions.**

Note:

**An outcome on its own does not prove that a decision caused it.**

Three operating rules:

### Fit the farm
**Start with the records a farm already has. Add automation where it genuinely reduces work; do not assume new machinery, perfect connectivity or constant manual entry.**

### Quality before intelligence
**Check completeness, consistency, duplicates, anomalies, freshness, provenance and representativeness before trusting a model or benchmark.**

### Keep uncertainty visible
**Known economics stay explicit. Forecasts and scenarios should show assumptions and ranges when evidence supports them, rather than pretending the future is certain.**

Boundary:

**These are PROFIT development principles, not a claim that the current Field Profitability build already includes telemetry, forecasting or optimisation.**

# 07 — Economic value & evidence

Eyebrow:

**Economic value · evidence**

Title:

**A value counts only when the evidence supports it**

Lead:

**PROFIT separates what is hypothetical, modelled, observed, attributed and verified. The label should never outrun the evidence.**

Evidence ladder:

**Hypothetical → Modelled → Observed → Attributed → Verified**

Confidence:

**Assessed confidence reflects the data behind a value — including completeness, consistency, freshness, provenance and representativeness.**

Not assessed:

**No assessment was made. “Not assessed” is not a confidence level.**

Verification chain:

**Baseline → Counterfactual → Intervention → Actual outcome → Incremental effect → Attribution → Confidence**

Trust line:

**PROFIT does not treat a forecast as a fact, a modelled benefit as an outcome, or an outcome as proof that PROFIT caused it.**

Current-site note:

**Nothing on this site is labelled Verified.**
