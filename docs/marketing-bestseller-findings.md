# Marketing Bestseller Findings — PROFIT Website

Research date: 2026-09-26

This document captures marketing principles from major bestselling books and current official author/publisher materials that are especially useful for PROFIT's public presentation website.

The goal is not to copy marketing formulas mechanically. The goal is to improve:
- farmer comprehension;
- trust;
- qualified pilot interest;
- investor credibility;
- differentiation;
- memorability;
- conversion quality.

## 1. April Dunford — Obviously Awesome

Core principle:

**Positioning comes before messaging, design and conversion tactics.**

A strong positioning process clarifies:
- competitive alternatives;
- differentiated capabilities;
- customer value;
- best-fit customers;
- market/category;
- relevant trends.

### PROFIT implication

Before locking homepage copy, explicitly define:

**What would a farmer do instead of using PROFIT?**

Potential categories to investigate with evidence:
- spreadsheets/manual calculations;
- accountant/controller analysis;
- farm-management software;
- agronomy platforms;
- consultant advice;
- intuition/experience;
- doing nothing.

Do not write competitive messaging until these alternatives are validated with farmers.

The homepage should make it obvious why PROFIT is meaningfully different from the alternative, not merely describe features.

### Rule

**No generic claim without a comparison frame.**

"Better farm decisions" is weak unless the visitor understands better than what, for whom, and why.

## 2. April Dunford — Sales Pitch

Core principle:

A strong sales narrative is not a feature walkthrough. It creates a structured story that makes differentiated value obvious.

### PROFIT implication

The website should behave more like a strong sales conversation:

1. establish the farmer's current problem/context;
2. show why existing approaches are insufficient;
3. introduce the insight that changes how the problem is understood;
4. show the PROFIT approach;
5. demonstrate value;
6. provide proof;
7. make the next step clear.

### Rule

Do not start homepage storytelling with a module list.

Start with:
**economic decision problem → new insight → PROFIT mechanism → evidence → action**

## 3. Donald Miller — Building a StoryBrand 2.0

Core principle:

**The customer is the hero. The brand is the guide.**

The classic structure is:
- character;
- problem;
- guide;
- plan;
- call to action;
- success;
- failure/what is at stake.

### PROFIT implication

Farmer = hero.

PROFIT = guide.

Avoid:
- long founder/company-first hero messaging;
- "we are revolutionizing agriculture";
- making technology the protagonist.

Prefer:
- what the farmer wants;
- what makes the decision difficult today;
- a clear path;
- visible economic outcome.

### Website mapping

**Character**
Farmer/farm business trying to improve profitability.

**Problem**
Farm data exists, but economic consequences of decisions are fragmented or unclear.

**Guide**
PROFIT helps connect operational reality with economic meaning.

**Plan**
Connect/enter data → understand economics → act → measure outcome.

**CTA**
Join the pilot / See how PROFIT works.

**Success**
Greater economic visibility and better-informed decisions.

### Rule

Every major section should answer:
**How does this help the farmer make progress?**

## 4. Chip & Dan Heath — Made to Stick

The SUCCESs framework:
- Simple;
- Unexpected;
- Concrete;
- Credible;
- Emotional;
- Stories.

### PROFIT application

#### Simple
Reduce the core idea to one memorable message.

Candidate:
**Turn farm data into more profitable decisions.**

#### Concrete
Use:
- field names;
- hectares;
- €/ha;
- operation costs;
- yield;
- weather;
- margin;
- screenshots;
- maps.

Avoid abstraction such as:
"optimize agricultural performance using advanced intelligence."

#### Credible
Use:
- evidence state;
- methodology;
- data provenance;
- confidence;
- real team expertise;
- real product screens;
- external/primary data sources when relevant.

#### Emotional
For farmers, the emotional outcome is not "AI excitement".

More credible emotional benefits:
- control;
- confidence;
- reduced uncertainty;
- knowing where money is going;
- being able to explain a decision.

#### Stories
Use short decision stories:
**Situation → Decision → Economic expectation → Outcome → Evidence**

### Rule

Every important claim should become either:
- more concrete;
- more credible;
- or easier to remember.

## 5. Marcus Sheridan — They Ask, You Answer

Core principle:

**Answer the questions buyers are actually asking, including difficult questions competitors avoid.**

The framework emphasizes radical transparency as a trust advantage.

### PROFIT implication

The site should not hide difficult farmer questions.

Potential high-value website questions:

- What data does PROFIT need?
- Can PROFIT work with old machinery?
- What if my farm data is incomplete?
- How accurate are the calculations?
- How is economic value calculated?
- What does "verified" mean?
- Who owns my data?
- Can PROFIT access my data without permission?
- How long does setup take?
- What integrations are required?
- What does PROFIT not do?
- What happens when confidence is low?
- How much will it cost? (when pricing is mature enough to publish responsibly)

### Rule

**Trust can be created by answering questions competitors prefer not to answer.**

Do not create an FAQ filled only with easy marketing questions.

## 6. Marcus Sheridan — Endless Customers

Current framework:
1. Say what others won't.
2. Show what others won't.
3. Sell in a way others don't.
4. Be more human.

### PROFIT implication

#### Say what others won't
Explain:
- limitations;
- confidence;
- attribution;
- data quality requirements;
- when a recommendation should not be trusted.

#### Show what others won't
Show:
- methodology;
- actual product workflow;
- how calculations are formed;
- evidence levels;
- example data lineage.

#### Sell in a way others don't
Educate farmers before asking for commitment.

#### Be more human
Use:
- real team;
- real farms;
- clear language;
- real limitations;
- direct contact.

### Rule

**Transparency should be a brand advantage, not merely a compliance section.**

## 7. Marcus Sheridan — The Visual Sale

Core principle:

Buyers increasingly want to see, not just read, how a product works.

### PROFIT implication

Critical concepts should have visual proof.

High-value visual content:
- short real product walkthrough;
- field → economics transformation;
- data-source explanation;
- old-machinery data collection example;
- VEV methodology animation;
- farmer workflow.

Video should explain complexity, not decorate the hero.

### Rule

If a complex claim can be demonstrated visually in 20–60 seconds, consider showing it instead of adding another paragraph.

## 8. Seth Godin — This Is Marketing

Core principles especially relevant to PROFIT:
- solve the customer's problem, not the company's marketing problem;
- build trust and permission;
- decide who the product is for and who it is not for;
- start with a smallest viable market;
- use empathy rather than attention tricks.

### PROFIT implication

Do not make the homepage try to speak equally to:
- all crop farms;
- all livestock farms;
- consultants;
- banks;
- agronomists;
- investors;
- machinery dealers;
- government;
- every country.

The first public narrative should center on the strongest current wedge.

The first production module is Field Profitability, so the website can use that as the clearest concrete proof of the broader vision while still describing PROFIT as a larger platform.

### Rule

**Specificity builds credibility.**

Trying to look universal too early can make PROFIT look vague.

## 9. Seth Godin — Purple Cow

Core principle:

Marketing cannot compensate for an unremarkable product.

### PROFIT implication

Do not try to manufacture differentiation with:
- unusual cursor;
- 3D globe;
- neon gradients;
- clever slogans.

The remarkable part should come from what PROFIT actually does.

Potentially ownable distinction:

**Farm reality → evidence → economic meaning → measured outcome**

and eventually:

**Verified Economic Value**

### Rule

**The website should reveal a remarkable product truth, not simulate remarkability through effects.**

## 10. Robert Cialdini — Influence

Relevant ethical persuasion principles:
- authority;
- social proof;
- reciprocity;
- consistency;
- liking;
- scarcity;
- unity.

### PROFIT application

#### Authority
Use real:
- agricultural expertise;
- economics expertise;
- engineering competence;
- methodology;
- credible data sources.

Do not use authority theater.

#### Social proof
When available, use proof from comparable farms:
- farm type;
- geography;
- production scale;
- evidence period.

Generic logo walls are weaker than relevant peer evidence.

#### Reciprocity
Provide something genuinely useful before asking for a meeting.

Possible future assets:
- profitability readiness assessment;
- economic-data checklist;
- field profitability template;
- educational calculator.

Any calculator must clearly distinguish modelled output from verified value.

#### Scarcity
Only use if true.

Example:
"Pilot cohort: 10 farms"
only if capacity is genuinely limited.

Never use fake countdown timers or invented urgency.

#### Unity
Farmer language, real agricultural situations and shared practical concerns can create stronger identification than generic startup copy.

### Rule

Use persuasion to reduce uncertainty, never to exploit it.

## 11. Jonah Berger — Contagious

Core idea:

People share ideas with social currency, practical value, emotion, public visibility, triggers and stories.

### PROFIT implication

The website itself is not primarily a viral product, but some content can be designed to travel.

Potential shareable assets:
- field profitability benchmark graphics;
- cost breakdown visuals;
- "what every hectare is worth" examples;
- decision case studies;
- transparent methodology diagrams;
- farmer-economic checklists.

### Rule

Create useful artifacts worth sharing, not "viral" gimmicks.

# Strongest combined marketing insight

The PROFIT website should not function like a brochure.

It should function like a **self-guided high-trust sales conversation**.

The visitor should move through:

**This is my problem**
→
**I understand why my current approach may be insufficient**
→
**I understand what PROFIT does differently**
→
**I can see how it works**
→
**I believe the claims are appropriately supported**
→
**I understand the limitations**
→
**I trust the company**
→
**I know the next step**

# Revised homepage marketing architecture

## 01 — Immediate farmer value

**Turn farm data into more profitable decisions.**

Make the outcome clearer than the technology.

## 02 — The current problem

Show concrete decision questions:
- Which field is actually profitable?
- Where are costs increasing?
- What is causing margin loss?
- What decision is worth changing?

## 03 — Why the existing approach breaks

Do not attack competitors.

Explain fragmentation:
- production data;
- operational data;
- cost data;
- external conditions;
- economic interpretation.

## 04 — The key insight

**Farm data only becomes valuable when it changes a decision and the economic effect can be measured.**

This is a strong candidate for PROFIT's central point of view.

## 05 — How PROFIT works

**Data → Economic interpretation → Decision → Action → Measurement**

## 06 — Product proof

Show Field Profitability.

Do not lead with a giant future feature list.

## 07 — Hard questions / objection handling

Answer:
- data quality;
- setup;
- old machinery;
- data ownership;
- limitations;
- confidence.

## 08 — Evidence

Clearly distinguish:
- Modelled;
- Observed;
- Attributed;
- Verified.

## 09 — Why trust PROFIT

- methodology;
- team;
- privacy/control;
- real product;
- transparent evidence.

## 10 — Action

**Join the pilot**

with clear expectation of what happens next.

# Marketing copy rules

1. Farmer is the hero; PROFIT is the guide.
2. One core idea per section.
3. Prefer concrete economic language to abstract technology language.
4. Explain the alternative/current workflow before claiming superiority.
5. Answer difficult questions openly.
6. Never invent social proof.
7. Never create artificial scarcity.
8. Do not use "AI-powered" as the main reason to care.
9. Differentiate through economic decision quality and evidence.
10. Use real stories as soon as they exist.
11. Keep investor messaging separate from the primary farmer narrative.
12. Every CTA should state a real next step.
13. Every value claim must match its evidence level.
14. Optimize for qualified trust, not maximum clicks.

# New strategic opportunity: Trust Center as marketing

PROFIT's transparency can become part of acquisition, not only compliance.

Potential trust content:
- How PROFIT calculates economic value;
- Evidence levels explained;
- Confidence explained;
- Data ownership;
- Data sources;
- What PROFIT does when data is missing;
- What PROFIT will never claim;
- Security/privacy principles.

This could materially differentiate PROFIT from vague "AI-powered agriculture" messaging.

# Marketing acceptance test

Before launch, show the homepage to a farmer for 10 seconds, then ask:

1. What does PROFIT do?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. What makes it different?
5. What would you want to know before trying it?
6. What would you click next?

Do not explain anything beforehand.

If the answers differ materially from our intended positioning, improve the site before increasing traffic.

# Key hypotheses to validate

- Farmers respond more strongly to economic visibility than to "AI/intelligence" language.
- Transparency about uncertainty increases trust rather than reducing conversion.
- Field Profitability is a stronger homepage proof point than a broad multi-module platform story.
- Farmer-specific concrete examples outperform generic platform claims.
- A visible evidence/verification system improves both farmer and investor credibility.
- A self-education path reduces friction before pilot contact.

These are hypotheses, not established facts. Test them with real users.
